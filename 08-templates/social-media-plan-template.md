# Social Media Plan — Master Template

> **How to use this template.** This is the document skeleton for a client (or Jewell's own)
> social media plan, generalised from a proven build. Fill the bracketed fields, delete any
> section that does not apply, and keep the guidance blockquotes only while drafting — strip
> them before the plan goes to a client. Placeholders carry structure, never real names, figures
> or strategy. Language conventions, voice and compliance posture are set per client in the
> sections below.
>
> A social media plan is a **tactical sub-plan**. It executes the social channel strategy defined
> in a wider Marketing Plan and reads alongside a sister plan (local area marketing, or the
> client's equivalent). If those parent documents do not exist yet, note that and keep this plan
> self-contained.

## The role of social in the marketing mix

[Two or three short paragraphs. Where does social sit? Is it the primary acquisition channel, or
the channel that makes the others convert? State it honestly — a plan that over-claims social's
role sets a target it cannot hit.]

> A useful default: social is rarely the primary acquisition channel — search and referral usually
> do that work — but it is what makes those channels convert. Someone who finds the brand
> elsewhere and then sees a strong, considered feed is far more likely to act.

## Voice and tone for social

Social inherits the [client] voice defined in the Brand Plan and applies it within platform
conventions. Tone shifts subtly by platform; voice does not shift at all.

### Caption principles

- Open with a hook line — a single sentence that earns the read.
- Deliver value or feeling — teach something or move someone.
- Close with a soft CTA.
- [Sentence case throughout — not Title Case Headlines.]

- [Emoji policy — e.g. one or two natural emoji maximum; many posts use none.]

## Engagement protocols

| Touchpoint | Response window | Tone / approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DM enquiries | [Same day where possible] | Warm, helpful, route to booking |
| Comments on posts | [Within 48 hours] | Acknowledge thoughtfully; do not over-thank |
| Tags / mentions | [Same week] | Acknowledge; share to story if on-brand |
| Negative comment | [Within 24 hours] | Acknowledge calmly, take offline; never argue publicly |
| Negative review | [Within 7 days] | Reply, acknowledge, offer a path to resolve; refer to Crisis Protocol |
| Spam / hostile / unrelated | No reply | Hide or block as appropriate; do not engage |

> **Booking intent cools fast.** For any client where a DM can become a sale, a same-day window
> beats a next-business-day one — set the DM SLA to the shortest the team can realistically hold.

## Compliance and regulated claims

> Keep this section proportionate to the vertical. For an unregulated client it can be light. For a
> **regulated** client (health, finance, legal, therapeutic goods, alcohol, gambling), it is
> non-negotiable and this is the section most likely to carry real risk if skipped.

[State the standard: which regulator or code applies, and the language bar. The Brand Plan
documents the standard; this plan operationalises it.]

### Compliance checklist (applied before every relevant post)

- Does the caption claim to cure, treat, fix or guarantee any [regulated outcome]? → rewrite or remove.
- Does it name a specific [condition / regulated subject]? → review for safe framing or rewrite.
- Is a testimonial used? → confirm consent and ensure no [outcome] claim.
- Does it reference [regulated ingredient / product]? → use approved framing only.
- Are images of real [patients / clients] shown? → written consent on file.
- Is the post sponsored or paid? → disclose per [ACCC / ASA / platform] rules.

> Where any check is unclear, hold the post for review. No exceptions.
>
> **Highest-risk sub-topics deserve their own named guardrail.** If the client operates in an area
> under heightened scrutiny (e.g. fertility for a health practice), do not rely on the generic
> checklist — write a specific rule for it here.

## Crisis and sensitive-content protocol

Pre-think the likely scenarios so the response is fast when needed.

| Scenario | Response | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Negative review or comment | Reply within window; calm, acknowledging; take offline; do not delete unless abusive | [JP draft, client approve] |
| Community tragedy or public moment | Pause scheduled posts; assess tone; resume when appropriate ([24–48h]) | [JP propose, client decide] |
| Influencer / paid-post outreach | Politely decline unless deeply values-aligned and appropriate | [JP] |
| Trending controversy (political, cultural) | Do not engage unless directly relevant; default is silence | [JP + client] |
| Privacy breach risk (recognisable image without consent) | Pull the post immediately; review process; re-approve workflow | [JP + client] |

## Measurement and reporting

**Reported monthly**

- Reach and impressions per platform.
- Engagement rate (saves, shares, comments).
- Follower growth (with a note on quality, not vanity).
- Profile-to-website click rate.
- Leads/bookings attributed to social ([attribution source]).
- Top three and lowest three posts — with a one-line learning from each.

**Reported quarterly**

- Channel performance against KPIs.
- Content pillar performance — which pillars are landing.
- Format performance — reels vs carousels vs single posts.
- Recommended adjustments for the next quarter.

**Review cadence**

- Weekly: one-screen glance — posts published, engagement signal.
- Monthly: full report into the marketing dashboard.
- Quarterly: strategic review with the client; calendar reset.
